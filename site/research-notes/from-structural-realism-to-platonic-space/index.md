# When does a recurring pattern deserve an explanation?

<!-- release-target: 2026-05-13. -->

## From resemblance to a claim

One of the reasons to put morphogenesis, proof search, and synthetic worlds beside each other is the possibility that an explanation found in one might tell us something about the others. A recurring form gives us somewhere to look, but the interesting part begins when we change the system or the way we describe it and ask what remains of the relationship we thought we had found.

Structural realism ([Ladyman and Ross 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199276196.001.0001), [French 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199684847.001.0001)) offers one way to think about why those surviving relationships might matter. It directs attention toward the structure that our models preserve, giving us a philosophical reason to investigate recurrence while leaving the existence of a nonphysical space as a further question.

## What a Platonic interpretation would add

A Platonic interpretation adds the idea that the patterns belong to a structured nonphysical space, with physical bodies acting as ways of accessing or realizing them. Extending this picture from mathematical regularities to patterns with richer behavior is part of what makes it intriguing, and also what makes the relation between the proposed space and a particular physical realization so important to specify.

The empirical question is what this stronger account lets us predict or construct. A productive programme would identify relationships between possible patterns, specify how a change in embodiment exposes them, and test those relationships across representations.

## What the comparison must predict

When two systems behave similarly, we can try changing their embodiment or intervening on them to see whether the same proposed relationship continues to predict what happens. Varying the description matters too, because a pattern that disappears as soon as we change the measurement may tell us more about that measurement than about a structure shared by the systems.

To make the idea of a physical system pointing into a pattern space useful experimentally, we need to say what changes at the interface and what those changes should do to the available patterns. [Spivak (2022)](https://topos.institute/people/david-spivak/Levin20220607.pdf) offers polynomial functors as a formal language for state-dependent interfaces, while Levin’s [TAME framework (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10346) motivates comparisons of behavioral competencies across substrates. These give us ways to formulate the question, but we still have to establish which relationships survive across measurements and implementations.

A Platonic account needs to explain what follows from the proposed pattern space that we could not infer from the system-specific models alone. If an ordinary dynamical or control model makes the same predictions with fewer assumptions, the experiments have not yet distinguished the stronger interpretation.

The [lab’s investigations](/dossiers/) approach these questions through comparisons of form, usable alternative routes, and responses to disturbance. This draft concerns what we might infer from such comparisons; the experimental reports determine which relationships we have actually established.
