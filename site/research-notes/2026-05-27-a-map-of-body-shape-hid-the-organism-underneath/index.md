# What a shape map leaves out

_19–27 May 2026 · Cross-system morphospace analysis._

We wanted one map on which a Flow Lenia organism, an EmbryoMaker embryo and a real fish could at least be discussed in the same geometric language. Each specimen was reduced to twelve measurements of visible form: elongation, compactness, symmetry, coverage, boundary complexity and related properties. The resulting map contained 25,167 Lenia specimens, 859 EmbryoMaker snapshots and 232 fish.

Putting these systems together made comparison possible, but it also made the loss of information measurable. The fish data had its own anatomical coordinate system, EmbryoMaker recorded properties of a developmental simulation, and every Lenia body still had a rule and a dynamical state behind it. The shared map retained only what could be seen through the same twelve measurements.

The sampled collections contained persistent topological features in that common space. A loop surrounds a gap in the observations; it does not show that the missing forms are impossible. A separate Lenia experiment changes rule parameters around a closed path while carrying the evolving body state forward. The parameters return to their starting values; the state can retain a difference. These imposed parameter paths are distinct from the loops detected among sampled shapes, and a residual state difference must be compared with retraced paths and numerical refinement before it supports a path-dependent effect.

The broader comparison showed just how coarse the shared map was. For fish, distances in the twelve visible measurements only weakly agreed with distances in the conventional anatomical coordinates. Agreement was better for EmbryoMaker, whose native measurements also describe final form. It was weakest for Lenia: the correlation between visible similarity and similarity in its native dynamical measurements was 0.078, and local-neighbour overlap was only about three to four per cent. Two Lenia organisms could therefore be close in the shared shape measurements while differing in the native dynamical measurements. This comparison did not reconstruct their complete internal states.

One neighborhood of 256 Lenia specimens lay unusually close to the fish collection and showed larger preliminary state differences after closed parameter paths. None of the five densely retested groups from that neighborhood passed both transport controls. In the broader transport screen, the joint scale-permutation result was about 0.094; that is a separate comparison, not a significance test of the fish resemblance. The fish proximity alone does not establish a connection between anatomy and rule-transport effects.

The shared map was still useful. It let us place otherwise incomparable bodies beside one another, and then measure how much that comparison left out. The failed fish-near lead redirected the work toward paired descriptions: one for what the body looks like, another for the rule, developmental history and dynamics that make that body possible.
