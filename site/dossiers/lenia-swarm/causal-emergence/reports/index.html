Three questions about a developing body

These articles bring successive experiments together around the questions they answer. Each follows the result through its controls, limitations, and next discriminating test.

Read the short synthesis

What makes a developing body harder to change?

The same disturbance loses influence as development proceeds, even while related bodies become more different. Separating the disturbance delivered from the response it produces is essential to understanding why.

The same shape can contain different possibilities

Internal composition can redirect development while leaving the visible body initially unchanged. Transplanting that composition reveals a second constraint: what the receiving body can do with it.

Does measuring the whole help us change it?

An organization score may anticipate development without predicting recovery or selecting a better intervention. Prediction, target achievement, and an advantage over simpler controllers need separate tests.
