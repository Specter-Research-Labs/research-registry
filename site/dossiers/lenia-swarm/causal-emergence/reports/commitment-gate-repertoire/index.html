States Kept Responsive Reach More Diverse Futures

The hidden developmental state we learned to control determines how many different organisms the same later causes can still produce.

Comparing 64 futures without categories or thresholds

From each passage-4 state—open, closed, untouched, and blind-full—we exhaustively applied every ordered pair of eight later actions. Thirty-two steps later, we measured the continuous geometry of all 64 resulting fields.

The question was brutally simple: did opening the gate leave more genuinely different futures reachable?

The open state had about five more effective future dimensions than the closed state.

Information plasticity became developmental possibility.

We first found a causal-developmental gate. Then we learned to hold it open. Now the complete action map shows what the gate controls: the dimensionality, breadth, and causal organization of the organism’s reachable futures.

This is a concrete bridge from the Levin-style idea to Flow Lenia. A hidden informational configuration precedes morphology, can be causally manipulated, persists after release, and changes the space of forms that later interactions can produce.

Using the measured repertoire to choose interventions

Target a morphology

Choose a desired future form from the 64-map and ask whether gate-aware control can reach it more reliably than direct action alone.

Find the bifurcation

Increase gate-opening dose continuously and search for a sharp transition in future-map rank, memory, or topology.

Watch birth happen

Extend selected open and closed futures into full organism formation and ask which hidden map changes precede fission, identity loss, succession, or self-maintaining novelty.
