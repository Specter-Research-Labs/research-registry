Pulse Order Changes Body Shape Early in Development, but Not Later

We applied the same balanced sequence of opposing hidden-state pulses at six stages of development. It changed body shape when applied from passages 12 through 32, but not at passage 48 or 64. The transition therefore lies somewhere between passages 32 and 48.

01 / Response across developmental passages

The same intervention changes body shape early, then stops doing so.

When the canceling pulses came first and the amplifying pulses came last, the body ended larger at passages 12, 16, 24, and 32 than it did under the opposite order. At passages 48 and 64, neither order reliably produced the larger body.

02 / The fields still separate

The intervention still changes the state, but no longer changes body shape in the same direction.

The difference in body radius between the two pulse orders fell by 0.055 from passage 32 to passage 48. Over the same interval, the raw distance between the resulting fields changed by only 0.0038 Hellinger units.

At passage 48, the two pulse orders still produce measurably different fields, but neither reliably expands the body relative to the other.

The example is radiant-blob-1453, selected because its immediate responses at passages 32 and 48 were closest to the cohort median.

03 / Across organisms

The loss of the response appears across the cohort.

Thirteen of sixteen families responded more strongly from passages 12 through 32 than at passages 48 and 64. The exact progression varies by family, but the later loss is present across the cohort.

04 / What happens at passage 32

The body spreads farther while remaining consolidated.

At passage 32, putting the canceling pulses first produces a wider, more occupied body with fewer connected components. Total mass remains nearly unchanged, so the difference concerns how matter is arranged.

At this stage, pulse order changes how far the body spreads, how densely it occupies space, and whether its material remains connected.

05 / What remains at passage 64

The remaining effect concerns connectivity rather than body radius.

At passage 64, pulse order no longer changes body radius. Putting the canceling pulses first does, however, leave less mass in the largest connected component, and the component-count change exceeds the sideways control. This suggests a later effect on connectivity, although it is weaker than the earlier effect on body size.

06 / What this opens

The same intervention produces different physical effects as the body develops.

Early body-shape response

Through passage 32, pulse order consistently changes body geometry in the same direction.

Loss of the radius effect

Between passages 32 and 48, the radius difference disappears even though the two resulting fields remain far apart.

Later connectivity effect

By passage 64, the remaining difference appears in connectivity rather than radius.

The experiment shows a developmental change in response: the same ordered pulses alter body size early, lose that effect between passages 32 and 48, and may still alter connectivity later.

The next experiment should test every passage between 32 and 48, then exchange hidden composition between early and late bodies. That would locate when the radius effect disappears and test whether the change follows the receiving body or the transplanted hidden state.
