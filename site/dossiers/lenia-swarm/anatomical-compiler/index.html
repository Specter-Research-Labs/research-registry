Orbium is a Lenia creature that glides across its grid while the values under it keep changing, so its shape lasts only as long as its rule keeps rebuilding it. We handed a search one frame of it at step 600, the kind of rule and the starting cells, hid the three numbers that set the rule’s reach and growth, and the search came back within six percent of the native value on each, with a body that stays whole for another 600 steps it was never scored on. Reading a rule out of a body is the easy end of something we want, which is to write down a body and have a search find a rule that grows it and keeps it. Two larger cohorts show what such a target already carries. Across 8,155 Flow Lenia creatures, shape explains about half of how a body travels, nearly as much as how active it is, and across 160 creatures cut at their centre, complexity says almost nothing about repair.

1One frame of Orbium was enough to recover its rule

OrbiumNative Orbium (top) and the body the recovered rule grows (bottom), each frame centred on its body, from step 100 to step 1,200.A Lenia creature first described by Chan: a crescent-shaped body about twenty cells across that glides in a straight line while the values under it keep changing, so its shape lasts only as long as its rule keeps rebuilding it. is the best-known creature of Lenia, the continuous cellular automaton in which each cell grows or decays according to a smooth weighted sum of its neighbourhood [1]. Orbium’s native value is 0.15. and the growth width sThe growth width: how far the neighbourhood density can stray from m before growth turns into decay. Orbium’s native value is 0.017.. We gave a search the update law, the kernel shape, the growth function and Orbium’s starting cells, and hid those three numbers. Each candidate was scored by growing it from the same cells for 600 steps and comparing its whole field with the reference at step 600 (the field matchOne minus half the summed absolute difference between the candidate’s mass field and the reference, both scaled to unit mass and aligned at their centres of mass, minus small penalties for differences in total mass and in the number of occupied cells. A perfect match scores 1.). The search started from R = 10, m = 0.12 and s = 0.024, a rule that breaks the seed into scattered rings, and it ran 2,688 candidates across the full parameter ranges and then 1,152 more around the best region.

It came back with R = 12.94 (native 13), m = 0.1526 (native 0.150) and s = 0.0180 (native 0.017), within six percent of the native value on each number. The broad stage had already put the radius and the growth centre close, and the narrow second stage raised the field match from 0.878 to 0.969 while pulling the growth width in from 0.0194. The body the recovered rule grows stays coherent through step 1,200, twice the horizon it was scored on.

More of the history did not help. A control search scored on steps 100, 300 and 600 with the same two-stage budget returned R = 12.80, m = 0.1435 and s = 0.0185, each farther from the native value than the single-frame fit, although its body also stayed coherent through step 1,200. This is one organism and one control, so it does not show that single frames are better targets in general, only that here the two earlier frames gave the search nothing it could use.

For someone trying to specify a body, this is the easy end of the problem. When the kind of rule and the starting cells are known, one picture of the mature body pins its rule down, and the body that rule grows keeps going on its own past the moment it was checked. The rule family, the seed and the elapsed time were all handed to the search here, and a body we invent will come with none of them.

2Shape predicts how a body travels nearly as well as how active it is

A body that matches a requested shape could still move in any number of ways, so we measured six behaviours in 8,155 Flow Lenia creatures from one screen of 8,192 seeds and reduced them to principal axes. Flow Lenia moves conserved mass along a flow that the mass itself generates [2], and these rules couple two channels through ten kernels [3], so their bodies glide, spin, pulse and break apart in ways a one-channel Lenia rule rarely does. It holds 28 percent of the behavioural variation.. Together the two axes account for 90 percent of the behavioural variation.

Size and elongation carry most of it. Each explains about a third of the variation in path length on its own, and the eight measurements together explain 53 percent of speed and of path length, 45 percent of displacement and 74 percent of temporal variance. If you write down a body by its size and outline, you have already chosen much of how it will move. Shape leaves nearly half of the variation in travel unexplained, which is where an unusual gait would have to come from, and a search scored on shape alone has no reason to look there.

3More complex bodies do not recover better from damage

A body we can specify also has to survive disturbance, and an intricate body does not survive it any better. We ran 40 coherent creatures and 120 random rules from the same starting patch, cleared a 40 by 40 square at the centre of each at step 400, and measured how far occupancy, spread and the largest piece’s share of mass ended from an undamaged copy by step 1,000, which we call form driftThe mean relative difference between the cut creature and an undamaged copy in occupancy, radius of gyration and the largest piece’s share of mass, over the steps from the cut at 400 to step 1,000. Zero means the cut left no trace on these measures.. ComplexityHow poorly the body’s mass map compresses: the compressed size of the image relative to its raw size, averaged over four downsampling scales. What little link there is points the wrong way: the more complex a body, the slightly further it drifted (r = 0.16 across the 160). The coherent creatures were more complex than the random rules and drifted further after the cut (0.34 against 0.25 on average), and only 31 of the 160 landed in the high-complexity, low-drift corner.

An intricate body is no more likely to heal. If the thing you want has to survive being cut, the target has to say so, with the cut inside the test that scores it.

4The next search has to find the seed as well as the rule

Orbium was recovered with its starting cells handed over, and a body we invent arrives without them. The two cohorts add what any such target carries with it: its size and elongation already decide much of how it moves, and its complexity says little about whether it will repair damage.

Our bet is that a search over seed and rule together can start from a compact patch, grow a withheld target body, and keep it, so that the route into the body is found along with the dynamics that hold it. The test hides a creature’s rule and seed, searches both against one frame of the mature body, then changes the starting conditions and cuts the fitted body. The bet fails if the rules that match the frame reach it only from the one seed they were found with, or if the fitted body drifts after the cut further than the native creature does, because then a frame pins down a picture and leaves the body that should keep it unspecified.

If it holds, specifying a body means handing over a picture of it and the damage it has to survive, and letting the search work out where to begin.

5A body is the target of a rulebook

We ask whether different kinds of systems share principles of organization: how a body takes shape, how it holds that shape and what it does when something is taken away. Lenia is a model organism for this because it comes with its rulebook, and this report runs the question backwards, from a body to the rulebook that grows it. For Orbium, with the kind of rule and the seed handed over, one frame is enough, and the rule it yields keeps the body going long after the frame it was read from. A body, here, is something the rulebook aims at and holds, and one picture of that body says enough about the aim to recover the rule.

The two cohorts show how much more a target has to say once nothing is handed over. Shape fixes about half of how a Flow Lenia body moves and leaves the rest to variation a picture does not show, and complexity tells us nothing about the question our lesion test asks, which is whether the body keeps its future when part of it is taken away. A target that should survive damage has to carry the damage inside its own test.

Appendix

A.1Engine correction and rerun

Earlier versions of this work reported results from Flow Lenia simulations whose spatial-derivative step computed the gradient with its sign flipped and its axes swapped, in a Python port of the simulator and in our main engine before it was fixed. Under that step the median creature broke into twelve pieces, which decoupled how bodies travelled from their shape. Those results are removed, and the shape and movement analysis and the damage assay in this report were rerun on the corrected engine, LeniaCLI build 060e90a71ec1, with the analysis code unchanged and the harvest regenerated from the same rules and seeds.

A.2The withdrawn specimen search

A search that tried to recover the rule of corpus specimen 2876 is withdrawn. Its batched initializer grew each candidate from a different random starting field, so the apparent improvement over generations belonged to the starting fields and not to the rules.

A.3Orbium and its replay

Orbium runs in additive Lenia, a single channel on a 192 by 192 torus, with the native rule Chakazul O2(a) from Chan’s Lenia library [1]. Additive Lenia has no derivative step, so the correction above does not touch it, and replaying the native, broad-stage and recovered rules on the corrected engine reproduced all 900 recorded frames byte for byte. Both searches are evolution strategies with 96 candidates per generation, 28 broad generations over R in 9 to 17, m in 0.1 to 0.2 and s in 0.01 to 0.03, then 12 narrow generations around the best candidate. The three-frame control sums the field match over steps 100, 300 and 600, so its best score of 1.08 is not comparable with the single-frame score of 0.969.

A.4Measurements

The six behaviour measurements are mean speed, path length, displacement and centre velocity (each log-transformed) and the field’s temporal variance and energy, standardized before the principal axes are taken. The creatures are those of the screen that passed its persistence filters. Each shape fit is an ordinary least-squares regression of one behaviour measure or axis on the eight standardized shape measurements; the size and elongation groups are fitted the same way on their own subsets. Form drift and complexity are defined in their notes; the cut is a 40 by 40 square set to zero at the centre of the grid at step 400.

A.5Data

Every number and figure here is produced by the report’s figure script from the recorded runs: the two-stage Orbium inverse with its configs, histories and frames, the corrected-engine shape and movement regeneration with the harvest it read, and the corrected-engine damage assay with the Orbium replay. The source manifest lists each of these files with its size and SHA-256, and the generated figures with theirs. The figure summary holds the recomputed values behind the prose: the recovered rule numbers and their errors, the explained variances and fits, and the damage correlation and counts. The Orbium inverse summary records the native, starting and recovered rules of both searches with their scores.
