Recovering the rule behind a Lenia body

The middle panel below is Orbium at step 600, a Lenia creature that glides across its grid while the values under it keep changing, so its shape lasts only as long as its rule keeps rebuilding it. If this one frame were all you had, could you read the rule back out of it? We handed a search the frame, the kind of rule and the starting cells, hid the three numbers that set Orbium’s reach and growth, and started it from the rule on the left, which shatters the seed into scattered rings. It came back with the rule on the right, within six percent of the native value on each number, and the body that rule grows stays whole for another 600 steps the search never looked at.

Reading a rule out of one body is a simple case of something we actually want, which is to write down a body and have a search find a rule that grows it and keeps it alive. Orbium shows how much a single frame pins down when nearly everything else is handed over. The rest of the page asks what a target like that brings along with it: 8,155 Flow Lenia creatures show how much of a body’s movement its shape already decides, and 160 cut creatures show whether complexity says anything about repair.

01

One frame was enough to recover Orbium’s rule

Orbium is an additive Lenia system, so its rule is a kernel, a growth function and three numbers: the interaction radius R, the growth center m and the growth width s. We supplied the update law, kernel shape, growth function and starting cells, hid those three numbers, and scored each candidate by growing it from the same cells for 600 steps and comparing its whole field with the reference at step 600. The search started from R=10, m=0.12 and s=0.024, ran 2,688 candidates across the full ranges, then 1,152 more around the best region.

More of the history did not help. A control search scored on steps 100, 300 and 600, with the same two-stage budget, returned R=12.80, m=0.1435 and s=0.0185, each farther from the native value than the single-frame fit, although its body also stayed coherent through step 1,200. This is one organism and one control, so it does not show that single frames are better targets in general, only that here the two earlier frames gave the search nothing it could use.

For someone trying to specify a body, this is the easy end of the problem. When the kind of rule and the starting cells are known, one picture of the mature body pins its rule down, and the body that rule grows keeps going on its own past the moment it was checked. The rule family, the seed and the elapsed time were all handed to the search here, and a body we invent will come with none of them.

02

Shape predicted how a body travels nearly as well as how active it is

A body that matches the requested shape could still move in any number of ways, so we measured six behaviors in 8,155 Flow Lenia creatures from one 8,192-seed screen and reduced them to principal axes. Path length, displacement, and center velocity dominate the first axis, which we call locomotion; temporal variance and energy dominate the second, which we call activity. Together these axes account for 90% of the behavioral variation.

ActivityR² 0.610
LocomotionR² 0.552
TortuosityR² 0.157

Size and elongation carried most of it. Each explained about a third of the variation in path length on its own, and the eight shape measurements together explained about half of speed and path length (53% each, 45% of displacement, 74% of temporal variance). Across all 112 screens of the same harvest, four rule families with 8,192 seeds each, the medians were somewhat lower in the same order (45% for path length, 40% for speed, 36% for displacement, 73% for variance). These are in-sample fits, not predictions on held-out bodies.

If you write down a body by its size and outline, you have already chosen much of how it will move. Shape leaves nearly half of the variation in travel unexplained, which is where an unusual gait would have to come from, and a search scored on shape alone has no reason to look there.

03

More complex bodies did not recover better from damage

A body we can specify also has to survive disturbance. We ran 40 coherent creatures and 120 random rules from the same starting patch, cleared a 40-by-40 square at the center of each at step 400, and measured how far occupancy, spread, and the largest piece’s share of mass ended from an undamaged copy by step 1,000. Complexity is the multiscale structural measure from the same runs.

What little link there is points the wrong way: the more complex a body, the slightly further it drifted (r = 0.16). The 40 coherent creatures were more complex than the 120 random rules and drifted further after the cut (0.34 against 0.25 on average), and only 31 of the 160 landed in the high-complexity, low-drift corner.

An intricate body is no more likely to heal. If the thing you want has to survive being cut, the target has to say so, with the cut inside the test that scores it.

04

The next search has to find the seed as well as the rule

Orbium was recovered with its starting cells handed over, and a body we invent arrives without them. The corpus adds two things any such target carries: its size and elongation already decide much of how it moves, and its complexity says little about whether it will repair damage.

Our bet is that a search over seed and rule together can start from a compact patch, grow a withheld target body, and keep it, so the route into the body is found along with the dynamics that hold it. The test hides a creature’s rule and seed, searches both against one frame of the mature body, then changes the starting conditions and cuts the fitted body. The bet fails if the rules that match the frame reach it only from the one seed they were found with, or if the fitted body drifts away after the cut further than the native creature does, because then a frame pins down a picture and leaves the body that should keep it unspecified.

If it holds, specifying a body means handing over a picture of it and the damage it has to survive, and letting the search work out where to begin.

05

Every number traces back to a source file

The ledger records the source for every number and replay. SHA-256 identifies the exact bytes. The corrected-engine entries record their engine build, code revision, and frozen plan; the September Orbium entries include their exact configs and media.

Methods note. Earlier versions of this page reported results from Flow Lenia simulations whose spatial-derivative step computed the gradient with its sign flipped and its axes swapped, in a Python port of the simulator and in our main engine before it was fixed. Under that step the median creature broke into twelve pieces, which decoupled how bodies traveled from their shape; those results are removed, and the shape and damage measurements here were rerun on the corrected engine. A search on corpus specimen 2876 is also withdrawn, because its batched initializer grew each candidate from a different random field, so the apparent improvement belonged to the starting fields. Orbium uses additive Lenia, which has no derivative step, and its replay on the corrected engine reproduced every recorded frame exactly.
