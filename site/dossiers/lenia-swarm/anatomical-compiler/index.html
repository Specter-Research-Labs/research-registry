Recovering the rule behind a Lenia body

One mature frame of Orbium was enough for a search to recover its rule parameters close to the native values, once the rule family and the starting cells were known. Without that knowledge the problem is harder, and this report follows what a search needs to be given before it can find a rule that grows and keeps the body we actually want.

The desired body keeps its form through a continuous process of local change, so recovering it means finding dynamics that sustain the anatomy as well as an image that resembles it. We first fix a known rule family and ask how much of its hidden parameters one observed frame gives back. We then turn to a corpus of Flow Lenia creatures and ask what a body’s shape says about how it moves and whether it recovers from damage, since a compiler that targets a shape inherits whatever comes with it.

01

One mature frame was enough to recover nearby Orbium parameters

This case uses Orbium, an additive Lenia system. It lets us ask how much a mature body reveals when we already know the kind of rule that made it. We supplied its update law, kernel, growth function, and starting cells, then hid three numerical parameters: the interaction radius R, growth center m, and growth width s. Each candidate develops from those same starting cells and is compared with the reference body at step 600.

Fix the model

We fixed Orbium’s update rule and one-kernel geometry, then searched for the three parameter values. Other update laws and kernel layouts are outside this comparison.

The search starts from R=10, m=0.12, and s=0.024. These values disperse the initial cells into a ring and small fragments.

We repeated both stages with three frames

A control search used steps 100, 300, and 600. It returned R=12.80, m=0.1435, and s=0.0185. Its replay also remained coherent through step 1,200.

The single-frame run recovered closer values

Both runs used the same broad and refinement budgets. The three-frame run ended farther from the hidden values than the run scored only at step 600. This is one organism, so it does not establish that single-frame targets are generally better.

02

Shape predicted locomotion nearly as well as it predicted activity

Even a body that matches the requested shape may move differently, so we measured six behaviors in 8,155 Flow Lenia creatures from one 8,192-seed screen and reduced them to principal axes. Path length, displacement, and center velocity dominate the first axis, which we call locomotion; temporal variance and energy dominate the second, which we call activity. Together these axes account for 90% of the behavioral variation.

ActivityR² 0.610
LocomotionR² 0.552
TortuosityR² 0.157

Size and elongation carried movement. Fitted one behavior at a time, eight shape measurements explained 53% of the variation in speed and in path length, 45% in displacement, and 74% in temporal variance. Across all 112 screens of the same harvest, four rule families with 8,192 seeds each, the medians were 45% for path length, 40% for speed, 36% for displacement, and 73% for variance. Body size and elongation each explained about a third of path length on their own. These are in-sample regressions, not predictions on held-out bodies.

So a compiler that asks for a body of a given size and elongation is also, to a large degree, asking for how it travels. A target written only as a shape will drag a movement profile along with it, and a target that wants a shape with an unusual gait is asking for something the corpus rarely produces.

03

Complexity said little about recovery from damage

A body we can specify also has to survive disturbance. We ran 40 coherent creatures and 120 random rules from the same starting patch, cleared a 40-by-40 square at the center of each at step 400, and measured how far occupancy, spread, and the largest piece’s share of mass ended from an undamaged copy by step 1,000. Complexity is the multiscale structural measure from the same runs.

Coherent creatures were more complex than random rules and drifted further after the lesion, 0.34 against 0.25 on average, and 31 of the 160 fell in the high-complexity, low-drift corner. Complexity alone does not pick out a body that repairs itself, so a target that should survive damage has to say so directly, with the lesion in the objective.

04

Can the search discover how to grow the body?

We would like to specify a body whose organization can be reached and then sustained, so a compiler has to find both a route into that organization and dynamics that keep it going. The Orbium reconstruction shows how much a single mature frame constrains when the starting cells and rule family are already known. The corpus adds two facts about any target we write down: a body’s size and elongation carry much of how it moves, and its complexity does not tell us whether it will repair damage.

The next test should require the search to discover a developmental route: start from a compact seed, grow a withheld target body, and maintain its anatomy. Searching for the seed and rule together would make the starting cells part of what is recovered rather than what is given. Changed starting conditions and damage would then test whether the fitted dynamics keep producing and sustaining the requested body beyond the trajectory used to select them.

Methods note. Earlier versions of this page reported results from Flow Lenia simulations whose spatial-derivative step computed the gradient with its sign flipped and its axes swapped, in a Python port of the simulator and in our main engine before it was fixed. Under that step the median creature broke into twelve pieces, which decoupled how bodies traveled from their shape; those results are removed, and the shape and damage measurements here were rerun on the corrected engine. A search on corpus specimen 2876 is also withdrawn, because its batched initializer grew each candidate from a different random field, so the apparent improvement belonged to the starting fields. Orbium uses additive Lenia, which has no derivative step, and its replay on the corrected engine reproduced every recorded frame exactly.
