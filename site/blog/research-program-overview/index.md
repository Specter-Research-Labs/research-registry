# On The Point Of It All

Underneath everything we do here is the suspicion that there is a structured, non-spatiotemporal space of patterns, and that physical systems are less the generators of those patterns than they are interfaces, or pointers, into the space where the patterns already sit. Whitehead called that relation *ingression*. Levin, more recently, phrases it as truths of mathematics that become embodied and *haunt* physical objects, which we think captures the same idea pretty well. That is about as strong and as Platonist as a reading gets, and we are keeping it rather than retreating to something more defensible, even though most of the actual work is the weaker and falsifiable kind (morphospaces, perturbation traces, attractor catalogs, etc.), since the thing that actually pulls us in is the asymmetry between mathematics and the physical world, the sense that the dependence runs in only one direction: you can turn up a new physical law without changing a single thing about mathematics, but if you could reach in and alter some property of the mathematics itself, the physical world would have no choice but to move with it, and that is exactly what you would expect if the patterns were the upstream thing and the physics the shadow. The other half is just temperament: we would rather understand one of these systems from the floor up, building the morphospace and the analysis and the simulator ourselves, than import a result we cannot take apart, and morphogenesis is the richest thing we have found to do that to. The SPECTER acronym is even pretty literal about it, we are looking for **S**tructural **P**atterns in **E**xperimental and **C**ontrolled **T**heoretical **E**nvi**R**onments.

The empirical motivation, basically all of it, comes from [Levin's group](https://drmichaellevin.org/). Bioelectric state in a planarian decides whether the fragment grows a head or a tail ([Beane et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.092015)), transmembrane voltage induces ectopic eyes in Xenopus tadpoles that then wire into something the animal can actually use ([Pai et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.073759), [Blackiston and Levin 2013](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.074963)), and [Xenobots](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1910837117) and [Anthrobots](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202303575) self-assemble from cells that were never selected for those bodies and then behave coherently inside them. Levin's framing puts a lot of this under one program: [TAME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10346), the cognitive light cone, multiscale competency, [polycomputing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10675), and more recently the quantitative push in [Chis-Ciure and Levin (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-025-05319-6). The shared idea is that organisms are navigating spaces of anatomical, physiological, and behavioral states, and that the navigation is doing real work that the cell-by-cell parts list cannot reconstruct. In a lot of these cases you get more competence out than the history put in, the system does something it was plainly never selected to do. Whatever cognition is, it is clearly not gated by neurons, and whatever a "form" is, it is clearly not specified the way we keep assuming it is.

We are not really in a quarrel with that program, more like a follow-up to it. The spaces being navigated are still mostly black boxes for the cases we care about. The geometry, the metric, the barriers, and the basins are gestural in the literature, and almost never actually computable, which leaves the questions that matter most, what the space is shaped like, how a physical system reaches into it, and what governs movement through it, sitting unanswered. If the strong reading is even partly right, the same piece of structure should show up across substrates that have nothing else in common, things like the same damage-response shape in a regenerating tissue, in a proof search over Lean tactics, in a synthetic world like Lenia. That cross-substrate recurrence is, as far as we can tell, the only test that separates "this is a real piece of structure" from "this is a metaphor we got attached to", and it is the spine of what we are trying to do here.

## On "Platonic space", and why morphospace comes first

A morphospace is a configuration space, basically a system's degrees of freedom plus whatever dynamics put it in some regions more often than others, with its attractor landscape sitting inside it. Tame, local, a statement about *this* system and nothing about ontology.

What we are actually after is bigger than any single morphospace. A pattern that keeps reappearing across substrates that share no materials, no scale, and no encoding is more naturally read as something real each of them is pointing at than as a coincidence of their separate physics, which is just structural realism (Ladyman and Ross, *Every Thing Must Go*, 2007) held to honestly, granting ontological seriousness to structure that survives change of representation, scale, intervention, and triangulation across measurement channels, and granting none to structure that lives in only one formalism.

None of this is askable yet, because the spaces themselves are not computable for any system we care about. There is no morphospace for planarian regeneration, none for Lenia organisms past hand-curated atlases, none for proof search, so the question of shared geometry has nothing on either side of it to compare. That is the only reason morphospace comes first, not because it is the destination but because without it the real question has nothing to stand on, and most talk of "Platonic" anything skips straight past that.

## The probe worlds

We are coming at this through a few small worlds we can actually perturb. Each one was picked because it lets us ask something the others hide.

### Wonton Soup

[Wonton Soup](../../research-notes/wonton-soup-follow-up/) treats Lean proof search as a morphogenetic system. You damage a goal by blocking a tactic mid-proof and watch how the search re-routes, or fails to. What makes it interesting is that different provers already disagree about what a proof should look like, the same theorem grows visibly different proof-trees depending on which prover is the substrate, so a single Platonic theorem casts different shadows, and the question becomes what survives a block, the basin family, the tactic class, or only the final proof object.

### Lenia

[Lenia](../lenia-morphospace-report/) is the synthetic morphospace we actually get to see, and that is the point of it. We can read out the full state, run thousands of variants, define our own metric, and ask the questions the wet-lab literature can only gesture at, what the morphospace looks like when you compute it instead of cartoon it, whether the same phenotypic regions reappear when we change the physics, and how a Lenia morphospace lines up against morphology from an entirely different substrate. It is also where the cross-substrate bet already has a quantitative shape: the coarse trace, the dominant mergers in the persistent homology, looks substrate-independent while the fine trace is specific to the implementation, and the persistence level where one turns into the other is a candidate measurement of where substrate-independence ends and implementation detail begins.

### Material memory

[Material memory](../../research-notes/material-memory-without-a-controller/) is a physical substrate with only local, history-dependent updates and no controller, jolted rigid bodies on a vibrating plate. The question is whether mechanical state alone, no field, no genome, no agent, can carry information in a way that looks anything like a basin or an attractor. It is the lowest-substrate version of the question Levin's group asks at the cell level, whether a system can remember something about its trajectory without anything we would normally call a memory.

### Categorical morphogenesis

[Where tissues break](../../research-notes/where-tissues-break/) recasts Grodstein, McMillen, and Levin's closed-loop Turing tissue (2023) as a wiring diagram in Catlab.jl, with cuts as a typed operation on it. The worst cut turns out not to be the one that severs the most edges but the one that leaves a fragment too small to sustain its own pattern, and which cut that is shifts abruptly as the diffusion parameters change. That is a claim about the morphospace having a real boundary rather than a soft gradient, the kind of thing you can only state once the system has a compositional structure to point at.

Across all of these, we are asking whether the same damage-response patterns show up in systems that otherwise share nothing. (See also [structural realism](../../research-notes/from-structural-realism-to-platonic-space/).) We only add a new probe world if it lets us ask a question the existing ones hide.

## The tools

The language we reach for is category-theoretic, polynomial functors for systems whose interface depends on their state ([Spivak's notes for Levin](https://topos.institute/people/david-spivak/Levin20220607.pdf), [Niu and Spivak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00990)), lenses for bidirectional observation and update ([Spivak 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02202)), fibrations for talking about implementation without pretending every level is the same thing, and eventually sheaves for the local-to-global part. Most of this is still scaffolding. Polynomial functors and persistent homology are the only pieces actually doing work in the experiments right now, and if we already knew which tools the problem wanted we would simply be using them. We keep the language around because it forces us to say which interface we are perturbing, which state space we are observing, and which level is supposed to be doing the work.

## Kinds of spaces

</figure>

When we say "space" we are usually running together three different things. Configuration spaces, where a point is a state a system can actually sit in, a Lenia parameter setting, a pattern of gene expression, a planarian's bioelectric state, the kind of thing you can enumerate and perturb. Model spaces, where a point is a formal object in whatever framework you brought to the system, an attractor landscape, a Fourier basis, a pendulum's phase space, all of them downstream of a choice of formalism rather than of the system itself. And ambient spaces, where a point is a pattern that belongs to no particular system at all, the Fibonacci arrangement that turns up in sunflowers and pinecones and nautilus shells, Feigenbaum's constant showing up in dripping faucets and population dynamics and lasers that share no substrate, the gliders that were arguably already there in the rules of Life before Conway drew one. Platonic space, if it is anything, is an ambient space.

You climb from a configuration space to a model space by formalizing, fitting, predicting, and that step is just science. The step we actually care about is the next one, from there up to the ambient space, the inference that a pattern you keep recovering is not an artifact of your system or your chosen formalism but something each of them is separately pointing at, and that is the unreasonable-effectiveness step, the one that sits in conjecture territory. Everything else in this article is an attempt to earn a single inch of that one arrow for a single concrete pattern.

None of it gets earned by convergence alone, since a ball rolling into a bowl converges and a Boolean network converges, so settling into a pattern is cheap and on its own proves nothing. What we look for instead is structure that survives perturbation, that holds a macro-family while the micro-path changes, and that reappears across substrates with nothing else in common, and we keep ourselves honest by insisting the description of the pattern stay simpler than the system it is meant to explain ([Aaronson 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.1791)), since a reading more complicated than its target has explained nothing. There is a candidate way to make all of this precise in the categorical language, but it is still a schema rather than a finished test, so for now it shapes how we look rather than telling us what we have found.

Three questions sit at the top once you take the ambient space seriously. Whether it has any intrinsic geometry of its own, what makes some regions reachable and some forms stable while most of the space is never visited and some transitions never happen. How a physical system acts as an interface into it at all, and what decides which pattern a given arrangement ends up summoning rather than another. And whether there is any objective, in the active-inference sense or some other, governing how a system moves through it. We do not have answers, we have substrates where the questions can finally be posed precisely, which is more than the literature usually gives you.
